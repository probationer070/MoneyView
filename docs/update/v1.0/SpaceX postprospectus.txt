**Circularity warning — read this alongside Part C.3.** Converting $75B of proceeds into a share count requires an offer price, so a *pricing* input legitimately enters the per-share calculation. The distinction the code must enforce: an offer price may size the denominator (a mechanical share-count conversion) but may never touch the numerator (cash flows, growth, margins, WACC, terminal value). Keep them in separate functions with separate signatures so the boundary is visible.

## Part C — Conceptual modules

### C.1 TAM plausibility check

Purpose: prevent issuer-claimed TAM from entering a valuation as a default. His framing — this is the one number Silicon Valley has learned to game, because a large TAM stops investors asking questions.

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class TamClaim:
    segment: str
    issuer_tam: float                 # as disclosed, USD
    issuer_source: str                # e.g. "S-1 p.112"
    category_current_revenue: float | None   # actual revenue of the named category today
    analyst_tam: float | None         # REQUIRED before valuation proceeds
    penetration_rationale: str | None # why the served subset < category

GLOBAL_CORPORATE_REVENUE = 140_000e9   # 2025, his stated benchmark; refresh annually
```

Flags, in ascending severity:

| Rule | Threshold | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `issuer_tam / GLOBAL_CORPORATE_REVENUE` | > 1% | `warn` |
| same | > 5% | `severe` (SpaceX AI: 18.6%; total claim: 20%) |
| `issuer_tam / category_current_revenue` | > 3× | `warn` — category inflation (Airbnb: ~6×) |
| `issuer_tam` equals a whole category while the firm serves a technically distinct subset | boolean | `severe` — demand a penetration haircut (satellite ≠ all broadband) |
| `analyst_tam is None` | — | **block**: refuse to value |

The last rule is the point of the module. Issuer TAM is stored, displayed and diffed, but is never a usable input — the same posture as `no_sector_pe` refusing to publish rather than guessing.

Empirical prior for the override, *derived from his three haircuts (27%, 10%, 13%) — not his stated rule*: issuer TAM × 0.10–0.30 is a defensible starting band for a consumer/enterprise technology claim. Surface it as a suggestion with the derivation shown, never as a default that auto-populates.

Also worth encoding as a display rule: report claimed TAM as a percentage of global corporate revenue next to the raw figure. "$28 trillion" does not read as absurd; "20% of all revenue earned by every company on earth" does.

### C.2 Life-cycle input weighting

Purpose: make the engine's screens conditional on life-cycle stage. This is his central methodological claim in this session — the informational value of a financial statement shifts across the corporate life cycle, and applying mature-company diagnostics to a young company is "not just myopic, it's often misleading."

| Stage | Questions that matter | Informative inputs | Inputs that are noise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Idea / start-up | Is the product buildable? Is there a market? | Founder, exit plan, capital access | Everything accounting |
| Young growth | What are the unit economics? | **Gross margin and its trajectory**, cost dynamics, ownership structure | Earnings, ROE/ROIC, revenue level |
| High growth | Can growth be sustained, and at what cost? | **Revenue growth, reinvestment intensity, sales-to-capital** | Accounting returns, current margins |
| Mature growth / mature stable | Is the moat real? | **Operating margin, ROIC vs WACC, excess return spread** | — |
| Decline | Is liquidation worth more? | Divestiture value, distribution capacity | Growth inputs |

Concrete engine consequences:

1. **Gate the ROIC vs WACC value-creation test on stage.** MoneyView's existing ROIC−WACC spread is a mature-company diagnostic. For `young_growth` and `high_growth`, suppress it or mark it `not_meaningful` — a company still searching for a moat has no meaningful excess return, and negative or near-zero book capital produces garbage denominators. All three SpaceX segments classify as young/high growth; the firmwide accounting return in 2025 would have been meaningless.
2. **Substitute the early-stage diagnostic pair:** gross-margin level *and* trajectory, plus capital intensity (sales-to-capital or capex/revenue). These are exactly the two things he actually extracted from a 277-page prospectus. Everything else in the numbers section moved his value by a rounding error.
3. **Never suppress a valuation for negative earnings or negative FCF at early stages** — flag as expected, not as a defect.
4. **Bear-case classifier** (small, cheap, high value). Reject as life-cycle-inappropriate: "loses money", "negative cash flow", "revenue too small relative to valuation". Accept as substantive: TAM overestimation, competitive margin compression, reinvestment/capital-allocation risk, governance. His words: the money-losing argument is "both lazy and unconvincing," because of course a young company loses money.
5. **Prospectus-bloat corollary.** 277 pages plus a ~100-page addendum, against sub-100-page prospectuses for Apple and Microsoft in the 1980s. Page count has grown; information content has not. A parser should target a narrow extraction set — segment revenue, segment gross margin, capex, R&D, share classes and votes, use of proceeds, ARPU and subscriber counts — and ignore the rest rather than attempting general comprehension.

### C.3 Price vs. value separation

Purpose: structurally enforce the separation the DCF audit found violated (estimated value anchored to current market price via a hardcoded scalar).

**Hard invariant:** no market price, transaction price, private-round mark, offer price, or comparable multiple may appear anywhere in the intrinsic-value chain — cash flows, growth, margins, reinvestment, WACC, terminal value.

Permitted uses of price, exhaustively:

| Use | Where | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sizing shares issued from known proceeds | Per-share denominator only | Part B circularity warning |
| Price/value gap | Output diagnostic | Never an input |
| Regression beta | WACC | Prefer bottom-up/Hamada — already implemented — for pre-IPO and newly-listed names |
| Pricing-comp cross-check | Separate reported panel | Labelled *pricing*, not valuation |

**Regression test that would have caught the audit bug:**

```python
def test_intrinsic_value_is_price_invariant():
    base = build_inputs(TICKER)
    v1 = dcf(base)
    v2 = dcf(replace(base, market_price=base.market_price * 3.0))
    assert v1.value_per_share == pytest.approx(v2.value_per_share)
    # and the strong form:
    assert "market_price" not in dcf_input_dependency_graph(dcf)
```

**Pricing model, kept separate.** His account of how banks actually set the number, which is a *pricing* algorithm and should be labelled as one: anchor on the most recent private/VC mark ($1.2–1.25T two months prior) → apply a public-company premium (cash inflow, reduced failure risk) → deliberately underprice to honour the price guarantee. He predicted $1.5–2T from that chain alone, months in advance; actual was $1.77T. Worth implementing as `ipo_price_estimate()` precisely because it demonstrates that the banks' number is derivable without any valuation.

**Action layer — separate from the value layer.** A price/value gap is not a trade signal. His position: overvalued means don't buy, but does *not* mean short, because mood and momentum dominate over horizons long enough to ruin you. Encode an explicit abstain band and record his stated precedents as the reason it exists:

- Facebook 2012: $38 offer, his value $28–29, no purchase; $18 three months later — value reached, so patience was the correct action, not shorting.
- Uber 2019: overpriced at IPO on his numbers; $14 a year later under COVID.
- His own SpaceX stance: sit out the IPO, do not short, revisit on price change. Explicitly not a permanent judgment.

### C.4 Governance / key-man risk

Purpose: represent controller-concentration risk as a valuation input rather than commentary.

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class ControlStructure:
    classes: list[ShareClass]     # (shares, votes_per_share)
    controller_economic_pct: float   # SpaceX/Musk: ~42%  [VERIFIED]
    controller_voting_pct: float      # SpaceX/Musk: ~82–85%
    @property
    def wedge(self) -> float:         # 2.0x for SpaceX
        return self.controller_voting_pct / self.controller_economic_pct
```

**Implement as a scenario branch on capital allocation, not as a discount-rate premium.** Default to the scenario; expose the discount-rate route behind a flag with the trade-off documented. Rationale: a WACC add-on is a blunt instrument that penalises the good outcomes symmetrically, whereas the risk he actually describes is specific and one-directional — a controller who cannot be overruled invests real capital against an overestimated TAM and an overestimated competitive position.

Overreach branch mechanics for SpaceX AI:

- Reinvestment is sized to the **controller's** TAM ($26T) and implied revenue ambition.
- Margins and achieved market share stay at the **analyst's** estimates ($3–4T TAM, 25% margin).
- The gap is capital deployed at a return below cost of capital.
- Value destruction ≈ PV of excess reinvestment × (1 − ROIC_new / WACC).
- Trigger conditions to expose in the UI: capex growth persistently outrunning segment revenue growth; R&D growth outrunning revenue growth; a segment with deteriorating gross margin absorbing a rising share of total reinvestment. All three were observable in SpaceX's FY2025 disclosures — capex doubled, R&D more than doubled, both concentrated in the segment whose gross margin fell.

He is explicit that this is his dominant residual risk, and that the dual-class structure removes the ordinary check on it: "this is not a corporate democracy."

Scoring what this validates:

- **Value estimate: sound.** Actual shares outstanding 13.57B against his ">13B" forecast. TTM revenue $23.0B (+121.9%), net income −$8.89B, EPS −$2.27 — a money-losing, cash-burning growth company, exactly as characterised.
- **"$135 is too rich": correct directionally.** The stock traded below the offer price within seven weeks.
- **"Don't short": correct, and the more valuable lesson.** It went to $225.64 first — a 67% adverse move against a short taken at the offer. This is the abstain band in C.3 earning its place. A module that converted a −26% value gap into a short signal would have been right about value and destroyed.
- **Price never reached value.** Trough $104.83 vs value $96–100. Current price/value ≈ **1.55×**. Sell-side median 12-month target ~$222 (range $190–800) sits at roughly 2.2× his intrinsic value — a usable illustration of the pricing/valuation gap for the UI.

Use this as the module's golden fixture: the same input set must reproduce $96–100/share, must classify all three segments as young/high growth, must refuse the $28T TAM, must flag the 2.0× voting wedge, and must return `abstain` rather than `short` at a $135 price.
